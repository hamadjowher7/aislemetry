What is Aislemetry?

COMMON QUESTIONS

Retail audit software
questions, answered.

What is Aislemetry and how does it use AI?+

Aislemetry is AI-first retail audit software that connects business questions, audit design, store evidence, and recommended actions. AI supports audit methodology, visible product recognition, facing counts, and evidence-linked findings. Teams review uncertain observations and decide on follow-up actions.

What is retail audit software?+

Retail audit software helps brands and field teams plan store checks, capture structured observations and photos, and analyze retail execution across locations. Aislemetry adds AI-assisted audit design and evidence-linked insights to that workflow.

How does SKU counting work?+

Aislemetry Visual Intelligence is designed to identify visible SKUs and count their facings from shelf photos. Teams can compare the observed assortment with the expected range and review the linked photos to investigate gaps. Shelf observations reflect visible products at the time of the visit.

How is share of shelf measured?+

For a facings-based audit, share of shelf is your brand’s visible facings divided by the total visible facings in the audited category, multiplied by 100. For example, 20 brand facings out of 100 category facings equals 20% share of shelf. Use a consistent category scope across stores and visits to make comparisons meaningful.

Can Aislemetry support mystery audits?+

Aislemetry is designed to support retail and mystery audit programs by structuring questions, field evidence, and analysis around the business problem. The specific methodology is reviewed by your team before launch.

What goes into a pre-journey plan?+

A pre-journey plan can define the store scope and sampling, questions, metrics, required photos or other evidence, and the analysis approach before auditors visit stores.

How does Aislemetry use store photos?+

Aislemetry Visual Intelligence is designed to read store and shelf photos and structure observations such as product presence, facings, share of shelf, displays, and planogram compliance. Examples on this page are illustrative.
