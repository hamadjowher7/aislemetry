What is AI share of shelf measurement?

AI share of shelf measurement calculates a brand’s proportion of visible category facings from shelf photos. Aislemetry detects packs, matches brands using product references, and divides brand facings by all visible category facings. Unresolved facings stay in the total, and uncertain identities need human review. Clear photos and consistent category boundaries are essential for useful comparisons.

Inputs
Shelf photos, category boundaries, and reference product images.
AI process
Count visible facings, match brands, and calculate each brand’s percentage.
Outputs
Brand facing counts, category totals, and shares with uncertain identities flagged.
WHAT IS SHARE OF SHELF?

A clear measure of
visible brand presence.

Share of shelf compares a brand’s presence with the whole defined category. A facings-based measurement counts the front-facing packs shoppers can see, including repeated packs of the same product.

FACINGS-BASED SHARE OF SHELF

Brand’s visible facings÷ total visible category facings× 100

24 brand facings ÷ 60 category facings × 100 = 40%
Illustrative example.

Agree on the measure before comparing.

Choose the category, shelf area, and counting rules before capture. Include all visible category facings in the denominator, even when their brand is unresolved.

Linear-width share measures shelf width instead of pack count. Different pack sizes can produce different results, so width-based and facings-based percentages are not interchangeable. Share of shelf also does not establish sales share or market share.

HOW AISLEMETRY USES AI

From shelf photo
to reviewable measurement.

AI supports the repetitive identification and counting work. Your category definition and product references give that work the right context.

  1. Define the scope and capture the shelf

    Select the category and measurement method. Capture clear, complete shelf photos, with a consistent approach to overlapping images and repeat visits.

  2. Detect products and count visible facings

    Aislemetry uses AI to locate visible products and count their front-facing packs. Hidden units behind those packs are outside the facing count.

  3. Match brands and product identities

    Product catalogue references help match the visible packs to brands and SKUs. Blur, glare, small packs, and partial labels can leave identities uncertain.

  4. Calculate shares and review the evidence

    Group the facing counts by brand and divide by the category total. Keep uncertain matches reviewable and unresolved facings visible before using the results.

SHARE OF SHELF / QUESTIONS

Make the measurement
mean the same thing.

How do you calculate share of shelf?+

Facings-based share of shelf = brand’s visible facings ÷ total visible category facings × 100. For example, 24 brand facings out of 60 category facings gives 40%. Include unresolved category facings in the total and use the same category boundaries for every comparison.

How is share of shelf different from SKU counting?+

Share of shelf measures a brand’s proportion of category presence; SKU counting measures distinct products in the visible assortment. Four identical packs are one unique SKU and four facings. Explore AI SKU counting.

Does Aislemetry calculate share from sales data?+

No. This use case calculates facings-based share from shelf photos. It describes visible presence, not sales performance. Sales share requires sales data and a separately defined market or category.

What makes shelf photos useful for AI analysis?+

Clear, well-lit photos with readable packs and complete category coverage help identification and counting. Agree how to handle overlapping photos, cut-off packs, obstructions, and glare during the pilot.

How should teams compare stores or visits?+

Keep category boundaries and counting rules consistent. Review the underlying counts, unresolved identities, and photo coverage alongside percentages. A change in the category total can change share even when your own facing count stays the same.
